## Editorial boundary

The historical record is real-world 1980–1989 evidence. In the actual timeline Tito died on 4 May 1980; in POLITIKA's fictional world he survives until his **invented** assassination in 1984, followed by Unaligned concealment and a revelation in 1989. Any real event after that point is an *alternate-history reference point* and must not automatically occur on the same date in the game. No source is evidence that any of the four playable fictional factions existed or committed actual crimes.

Regional republics and autonomous provinces are political and administrative subjects, not universal military teams or interchangeable cultural tilesets. Avoid interpreting one population's historical protests as an innate propensity to rebel. Military, police, press, workers, political parties, religious groups, provincial bodies and federal institutions have distinct responsibilities. All gameplay ideas below are **suggestions**, not implemented mechanics. The six socialist republics are Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Macedonia and Montenegro; Kosovo and Vojvodina were autonomous provinces within Serbia, with legal standing contested in late-1980s reform disputes.

## Research qualification and major remaining gaps

1. **Exact dates and variants:** a month/year denotes the available precision; ranges often encode extended political processes. Do not render 1980s events with a made-up exact day.
2. **Kosovo:** casualty counts, legality and quorum of the March 1989 vote, and accounts of ethnicity-related incidents are contested. Use independent and multilingual materials; never assert unverified collective guilt. Rights organizations, contemporary state institutions and Albanian-affiliated accounts should be compared.
3. **1983 Sarajevo Trial and 1987 Agrokomerc:** first-pass sources include participant-affiliated historical writing and first-person testimony; events are documented but the causes, accusations and individual culpability must be cross-checked.
4. **Macedonia:** this version has contextual research on its republican experience, but no reliably item-dated standalone Macedonian political event from 1980–1989. It is deliberately marked *context-only* rather than inventing a local milestone; acquire Macedonian-language party proceedings, 1980s assembly records and newspapers for a proper next pass.
5. **Economy:** IMF retrospective narratives establish policy trends and dates, not visual accuracy for a factory or an exact in-game resource price. External economic actions have consequences, not an automatic winner/loser on a political axis.
6. **Period limit:** the Yugoslav League of Communists' 14th Congress, most republican multiparty elections, the armed Yugoslav wars and the break-up of the federation belong to **1990–1992**, not 1989. POLITIKA's 1989 finale is an intentional fictional acceleration.
7. **Copyright:** all these entries link to sources; no third-party photographs or article reproductions were uploaded. See [Rights and provenance](RIGHTS_AND_PROVENANCE.md).

## How to add an event

Use a stable `POL-YYYY-POL-...` ID and a source URL, historical start/end or approximate period, location and administrative scope, description, why it is consequential, evidence type, limits and proposed uses. Add it to `POLITICAL_EVENTS.csv` and `CATALOG.csv`, update [reference index](media/reference-only/INDEX.md), and revise story/chronology *only after* checking the 1984 survival/assassination and 1989 disclosure divergence. Historical entries are immutable evidence records rather than mutable faction lore.
