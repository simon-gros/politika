## Historical integrity

The archive separates maps **originally published 1980–1989** from **older maps** (useful for terrain and historical infrastructure), **later maps** (useful for topography but possibly anachronistic), and **collection leads** where the exact map or date has not been established. *Publication date*, *date depicted*, *revision date*, and *source collection date* are different. A map labelled 1983 by a present-day website is not automatically a map printed in 1983.

## Newly verified 1980s cartographic discoveries (29 October 2026)

In addition to the previously indexed CIA maps and Zagreb city map, the [expanded catalogue](MAP_CATALOG.csv) now distinguishes original period cartography, contemporary library-held publications and digitized source reproductions. See [Acquisition and RTS map-production guide](MAP_ACQUISITION_GUIDE.md) for an item-by-item access and rights plan.

- **1981–1982 Sisak TK50 military topographic map:** [Croatian History Museum VGI catalogue](https://kartevgi.hismus.hr/en/e-catalog/belgrade/) (HPM-102650) describes 1:50,000 Sisak 1 and Sisak 2 maps produced in 1981 and 1982, joined into a larger study of the Sisak, Petrinja, Glina and river network. Museum copyrights apply.
- **1988 Yugoslav Railways network:** [Wikimedia Commons image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yugoslavia_Railway-Lines_1988.jpg), [Spomenik Database interactive edition](https://www.spomenikdatabase.org/1988-map), publisher Jugoslovenske železnice, Geokarta Belgrade (1988). These are two source locations for the **same underlying map**, not two different period maps. The Commons official-work public-domain claim requires independent verification before redistribution.
- **1986 WWII memorial and tourist map:** [Spomenik Database scan presentation](https://www.spomenikdatabase.org/1986-map), attributed to Gojko Jokić and printed at 1:2,500,000. Historical monuments, wayfinding and public ceremonial geography inform mission scenery; the website's images are not automatically game assets.
- **1988–1989 Geokarta Belgrade city plan:** [CiNii scholarly library catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA75523445), 1:20,000, including municipal wards, eight insets, landmark buildings and public transport keys; original produced in 1988, paper holding catalogued, not an open scan.
- **1988 Yugoslavia road map:** [University of Maribor Library COBISS item](https://plus-legacy.cobiss.net/cobiss/si/en/bib/ukm/35263233), 4th edition published in Pula, 1:1,550,000. A substantial period reference for civilian transit and interstate routes, currently physical library access.
- **1982 Yugoslavia tourist map with eight illustrated routes:** [Austrian State Archives item](https://www.archivinformationssystem.at/detail.aspx?ID=2139619), produced by the Yugoslav Tourist Association. Archival consultation access does not imply scan redistribution permission.
- **1980, 1982, 1985, 1986 foreign-language tourist map editions:** individual [COBISS Serbia](https://plus-legacy.cobiss.net/cobiss/sr/sr_latn/bib/204791815) records; document multilingual road signage, visitor geography and print design, subject to individual edition comparison.
- **1989 Michelin road map no. 991:** [CiNii catalogue](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BC14808956), eighth edition at 1:1,000,000, issued in 1989 and copyrighted 1988.
- **Slovenian-language SFRY maps published in 1981, 1982, 1984, 1986, 1987 and 1989:** [COBISS search](https://plus-legacy.cobiss.net/cobiss/si/sl/bib/search?c=ar%3D7518819&r1=0). Some may be reprints of the same cartography and an author credit may outlive the original cartographer.
- **Official Croatian topographic TK100 and TK200:** [Croatian geodetic spatial-data catalogue](https://registri.nipp.hr/izvori/11) dates the 55-sheet TK100 series to 1980–1984 and the 18-sheet TK200 series to 1982–1986. This verifies **series production**, not an individual download of all 73 sheets.
- **Slovenian historical military topographic state 1985–1986:** [Slovenian cartographic history on dLib](https://dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-N9BMLCDV) describes retained VGI 1:25,000 reproduction materials; these can help research 1980s roads and settlement state without conflating them with newer Slovenian editions.
- **Serbian Military Geographical Institute map inventory:** [official former-Yugoslavia map holdings](https://www.vgi.mod.gov.rs/lat/221/karte-bivse-jugoslavije-221), covering eight scales from 1:25,000 to 1:1,000,000. Their individual dates and reproduction conditions require checking.

## Use in POLITIKA

**Politics and institutions:** reference the six Yugoslav socialist republics and two autonomous provinces without turning civilian populations into game combatant factions. The game's post-1984 Tito cover-up and 1989 disintegration are *fictional*; a real 1989 map does not prove either occurred.

**Terrain and architecture:** Dinaric mountain ridges, Slovenian Alps, Pannonian plains, Adriatic coastline, large river valleys, inhabited urban regions and topographic contours support credible RTS maps. Terrain can persist across decades; junctions, airfields, buildings, roads and industrial infrastructure require year-specific verification.

**Game systems:** rural roads, ports, ferries, airports, rail depots, bridges and checkpoints support logistics and trade, including the Meridian Pact's fictional mercenary/merchant functions. Urban map references support the Unaligned's neutral civilian traffic and administrative jurisdictions. Political maps should inform believable local/regional civilian government rather than assigning all decisions to a single centralized AI entity.

## Priority acquisitions and gaps

1. Locate authentic **1980–1989** editions of Yugoslav road/tourist maps, railway maps, political/administrative atlas pages, physical/school maps, cities and industry; verify original creator and print year.
2. Map the whole federation: **Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, Macedonia, and Kosovo and Vojvodina**. Use period place names: e.g. *Titograd* in actual 1980s Montenegro, while later maps label *Podgorica*.
3. Identify original plan sheets for **Belgrade, Zagreb, Ljubljana, Maribor, Sarajevo, Novi Sad, Priština, Skopje, Split, Rijeka, Niš and Titograd** and major border crossings. Modern M709 index entries are geographic leads, not proof of original 1980s urban street layouts.
4. Review every original item's licence before adding any map image, PDF, or GeoPDF to Git. Open web access is not a redistribution licence.
