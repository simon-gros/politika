# POLITIKA — Historical Map Archive (1980–1989)

**Research seed: 27 October 2026.** This is POLITIKA's cartographic reference guide, paired with [MAP_CATALOG.csv](MAP_CATALOG.csv) and the [reference-only map index](media/reference-only/maps/INDEX.md).

## Historical integrity

The archive separates maps **originally published 1980–1989** from **older maps** (useful for terrain and historical infrastructure), **later maps** (useful for topography but possibly anachronistic), and **collection leads** where the exact map or date has not been established. *Publication date*, *date depicted*, *revision date*, and *source collection date* are different. A map labelled 1983 by a present-day website is not automatically a map printed in 1983.

- **1981 CIA shaded-relief version:** [Library of Congress, LCCN 2011587054](https://www.loc.gov/item/2011587054/), base number 504661 (545723) 1-81.
- **1981 Zagreb city map:** [UT Austin, Department of State 1981 city-map listing](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/world_cities.html).

## Contemporary statistical sources requiring map-plate inspection

- [Statistical Yearbook of Yugoslavia, 1989](https://publikacije.stat.gov.rs/G1989/Pdf/G19892003.pdf).

## Yugoslav Military Geographical Institute / Croatian History Museum

- [1962 road atlas, partly updated in 1963, 36 sheets at 1:300,000](https://kartevgi.hismus.hr/en/karte/road-map-of-the-socialist-federal-republic-of-yugoslavia/), **HPM-102758**; includes roads, bridges, fuel and service points, town crossings.
- [1974 road map, sheet 1 of 2, at 1:500,000](https://kartevgi.hismus.hr/en/karte/road-map-sheet-of-the-socialist-federal-republic-of-yugoslavia/), **HPM-102649**; includes Ljubljana, Zagreb, Rijeka and Split road-plan insets.
- [Croatian History Museum VGI e-catalogue](https://kartevgi.hismus.hr/en/e-catalog/belgrade/) also lists a physical map associated with the SFRY hydrometeorological service; the item's date and scale require verification.

These older maps are excellent *baseline references* but do not independently prove 1984–1989 roads or bridges remained unchanged.

## Topography and supplementary cartography

- [Former Yugoslavia Series M709 topographic GeoPDFs, mostly 1:50,000](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/topo/former_yugoslavia/), with [sheet index map](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/topo/former_yugoslavia/index_map.jpg). - [1:100,000 Series M6010](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/serbia.html), [1:250,000 Series M501](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/slovenia.html), [1:500,000 Tactical Pilotage Charts](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/yugoslav.html), [1:500,000 British World Series 1404](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/yugoslav.html) and [1:500,000 M404](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/yugoslav.html) provide additional terrain and transportation references with mixed/earlier/later dates.
- [CIA 1992 former Yugoslavia map folio](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/yugoslav.html) and [1996 CIA former Yugoslavia shaded-relief map](https://www.loc.gov/item/96684084/) are *post-period comparison sources*. [Mapsland's purported 1983 political map](https://www.mapsland.com/europe/yugoslavia/detailed-political-map-of-the-former-yugoslavia-1983) lacks verified original issue provenance.

## Use in POLITIKA

**Politics and institutions:** reference the six Yugoslav socialist republics and two autonomous provinces without turning civilian populations into game combatant factions. The game's post-1984 Tito cover-up and 1989 disintegration are *fictional*; a real 1989 map does not prove either occurred.

**Terrain and architecture:** Dinaric mountain ridges, Slovenian Alps, Pannonian plains, Adriatic coastline, large river valleys, inhabited urban regions and topographic contours support credible RTS maps. Terrain can persist across decades; junctions, airfields, buildings, roads and industrial infrastructure require year-specific verification.

**Game systems:** rural roads, ports, ferries, airports, rail depots, bridges and checkpoints support logistics and trade, including the Meridian Pact's fictional mercenary/merchant functions. Urban map references support the Unaligned's neutral civilian traffic and administrative jurisdictions. Political maps should inform believable local/regional civilian government rather than assigning all decisions to a single centralized AI entity.

## Priority acquisitions and gaps

1. Locate authentic **1980–1989** editions of Yugoslav road/tourist maps, railway maps, political/administrative atlas pages, physical/school maps, cities and industry; verify original creator and print year.
2. Map the whole federation: **Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Montenegro, Macedonia, and Kosovo and Vojvodina**. Use period place names: e.g. *Titograd* in actual 1980s Montenegro, while later maps label *Podgorica*.
3. Identify original plan sheets for **Belgrade, Zagreb, Ljubljana, Maribor, Sarajevo, Novi Sad, Priština, Skopje, Split, Rijeka, Niš and Titograd** and major border crossings. Modern M709 index entries are geographic leads, not proof of original 1980s urban street layouts.
4. Review every original item's licence before adding any map image, PDF, or GeoPDF to Git. Open web access is not a redistribution licence.
