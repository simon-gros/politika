# POLITIKA — 1980s Yugoslav design, atmosphere and narrative reference atlas

**Purpose.** Translate the first [historical catalogue](CATALOG.csv) into useful art-direction briefs, environmental storytelling, source-based mission possibilities and **explicitly proposed** mechanics. It supplements rather than changes the authority of [Story Outline](../Docs/STORY_OUTLINE.md), [Concepts Outline](../Docs/CONCEPTS_OUTLINE.md), [Propaganda and Morale](../Docs/PROPAGANDA_AND_MORALE.md), [Resource Economy](../Docs/RESOURCE_ECONOMY.md) and [Unaligned Counterintelligence](../Docs/UNALIGNED_COUNTERINTELLIGENCE.md).

**Design identity:** late-socialist Yugoslavia, a geographically diverse federation with self-managed factories, complicated federal administrations, internationally visible modernism, industrial transport, ordinary civilian life and rival political cultures. The central visual idea is *normality under systemic pressure*: a working tram and crowded newspaper kiosk can stand fifty metres from an unmarked restricted government building. Avoid portraying the whole country as a uniformly abandoned brutalist battlefield, a Soviet republic, or a one-note Cold War caricature.

These briefs are design interpretations of references, not direct reproductions or factual claims about entire cities. **No item below is an instruction to import a protected photograph, emblem, poster, soundtrack, corporate brand or museum image.**

## 1. Sarajevo 1982–1984: Olympic modernism alongside ordinary infrastructure

*Research:* `POL-1982-SAR-TRAM-001`–`004`, `POL-1983-SAR-HOTEL-001`, `POL-1984-SAR-GAMES-001`, `POL-1984-SAR-BRANDING-001`, `POL-1984-SAR-VUCKO-001`.

*Observable motifs:* red-and-cream trams, catenary wires, railway paving, street-front buildings, late-modernist concrete/glass hotel masses, distinctive geometric facades, bright public-event wayfinding, mountain venues and snow infrastructure. The dated 1982 Commons photographs are particularly strong for actual traffic geometry and surface details. Modern images of restored venues are not proof of their 1984 appearance.

*Art proposal:* layered city tile family: older streets and tram tracks; newer hotel/office structures; temporary festival pennants and modular wayfinding; a thick winter snow variant with gritted crossroads, wet tracks and exposed tram cable. Work out original symbols rather than copy the Olympic rings, official snowflake or Vučko. Proposed accent palette: muted cream / tram red / concrete gray, with occasional vivid yellow or cold-blue public signage. The *palette is a synthesis*, not a paint specification obtained from a single archive item.

*World / mission proposals:* civilian transport circulates independently of combat, an athlete/delegation convoy gets priority at a crossing, and an official TV vehicle competes for a narrow street with a military transport. Public-event broadcasting creates civilian visibility that complicates a secret surveillance mission. The actual 1984 Games may need chronology adaptation because of POLITIKA's assassination year.

## 2. Federal communications and newspapers: the surface of legitimacy

*Research:* `POL-1984-MLADINA-001`, `POL-1985-KATEDRA-001`, `POL-1986-MLADINA-001`, `POL-1988-MUSIC-PRESS-001`, `POL-1987-POSTER-001`.

*Observed evidence:* dated magazine/newspaper entries and associated publication metadata. The 1985 `Katedra` issue is scan-listed; two `Mladina` issue records currently indicate restricted on-site access. Do not describe their typography, front-page headlines or specific editorial stance as inspected unless the page itself has been verified.

*Art proposal:* original newspaper kiosk stack, printshop and station newsboard; serif and condensed sans-serif print hierarchies; monochrome official forms set against colourful locally produced youth magazines; carbon copies, fax/telex-style traffic where supported by a separate period reference, voice tapes and physical master reels. Create fictitious titles and article text for release. Avoid modern UI idioms such as web dashboards, mobile push alerts, desktop notifications, QR-code surveillance or broadband streams.

*World / mission proposals:* `Printed statement` and `broadcast recording` are distinct evidence carriers; one may contradict the other. A newsstand can be an inexpensive, time-gated civilian signal-source without being a universal listening post. A broadcast master offers a date and studio location but is not by itself proof that Tito was dead; independently verify it against other canonical evidence.

## 3. Ritual design and public consent

*Research:* `POL-1987-DAY-YOUTH-001`, `POL-1987-POSTER-001`, `POL-1984-SAR-BRANDING-001`, `POL-1989-NAM-001`.

*Observed evidence:* museum-item description of a 1987 relay baton; historically contested graphic design; real Non-Aligned summit documents. The 1987 poster was *not* actually hung on city walls.

*Art proposal:* two separate original visual grammars: formal federal certificates, stamps, banners and standardized conference folders; dissenting youth posters with more experimental juxtaposition, collage, distorted type and regional idioms. Avoid reproducing the historical 1987 appropriation of a Nazi image or presenting the artwork as a canonical fictitious in-game poster. A highly controlled portrait broadcast should appear polished and bureaucratic rather than overtly sinister.

*World / mission proposals:* state ceremonial schedules create safe routes and public witnesses. Propaganda raises a claim's reach without increasing its truth. Counter-narratives can lower trust without making every civilian rebellious. The post-1980 historical rituals are especially sensitive to POLITIKA's living-Tito counterfactual.

## 4. Industrial Yugoslavia: production with people attached

*Research:* `POL-1982-DEBT-001`, `POL-1989-INFLATION-001`, source leads at the Archives of Yugoslavia and regional state/enterprise archives. **Evidence gap:** factories' specific 1980s shopfloors and vehicles need item-level period photos and manuals before modelling them as documented.

*Art proposal:* machine halls, outdoor loading bays, power substations, freight sidings, chimneys, water towers, workers' entrances, posted shift rotas, maintenance sheds, safety marking and materials storage. Different site silhouettes should reflect production function and region, not a faction's unrelated visual branding.

*Gameplay link:* [Resource Economy](../Docs/RESOURCE_ECONOMY.md) can use historically grounded categories of industrial flows (rail freight, ports, energy distribution, retail supply). Workers' self-management is more complex than a universal centrally scripted factory; proposed policy/union interactions can shape access and morale without making all industry playable faction property.

*Mission seed:* a neutral factory refuses an unscheduled strategic shipment unless authorized; the player can negotiate, reroute, or deliberately violate access rules and incur costs. This is a fictional system hook.

## 5. The inhabited neutral state: Unaligned civilian and military worlds

*Research:* `POL-1984-USOC-001`, period press, Olympics administration, `POL-1988-JBTZ-001`; military/municipal manuals remain a key gap.

*Art proposal:* each neutral compound has a mundane function first—registry offices, schools, hospitals, technical depots, police checkpoints, military barracks and railway administration. Signage changes by republic, jurisdiction and language. Civilian clerks and technical staff have separate silhouettes from soldiers. Restricted presidential facilities can be almost boring from the street: guarded service gates, not giant villain bases.

*Gameplay link:* the [Concepts Outline](../Docs/CONCEPTS_OUTLINE.md) assigns Unaligned ordinary administration, police, JNA, territorial units, civilians and a *small* hidden continuity inner circle; they should not all share secret information. Its counterintelligence deception is a **game fiction**, not a documented feature of Yugoslav public institutions.

*Mission seed:* a neutral traffic marshal enforces a road diversion at a tram reconstruction site while an Unaligned covert cell targets a specific sealed document. Ordinary neutral AI is not automatically complicit.

## 6. Economic pressure, consumer life and the black market

*Research:* `POL-1982-DEBT-001`, `POL-1989-INFLATION-001`, `POL-1985-KATEDRA-001`, `POL-1980-1989-ARCHIVE-YU-001`.

*Evidence-based context:* debt-service problems, unemployment, currency devaluation and rapidly accelerating inflation. These are historical trends; no single item gives an exact historical game-resource price or a specific black-market network.

*Art proposal:* a sequence of shop shelves, ration-style noticeboards only where verified, queueing civilians, everyday packaging, handwritten new-price signs and altered transportation density. Make markets visibly necessary to citizens, not merely procurement menus for commanders.

*Gameplay link:* The Meridian Pact's Black Market is fictional mercenary infrastructure. It can exploit pressure points via the [Resource Economy](../Docs/RESOURCE_ECONOMY.md), but should not be portrayed as synonymous with real ethnic or political groups. Its stock appears as mechanically plausible 1980s equipment unless an explicit alternate-history technology is specified.

## 7. Kosovo and the mining interior: a political site, not a generic war zone

*Research:* `POL-1988-KOSOVO-001` and `POL-1989-TREPCA-001`.

*Supported focus:* mining labour, underground workings, the Trepča site and the historically documented 1988 miners' political march and 1989 strike. The online oral histories give retrospective workers' voices; period photo/technical documentation is still needed for precise geometry.

*Art proposal:* external minehead facilities, ore haulage, worker welfare infrastructure, ventilation and tunnels, industrial lighting; seek dated Trepča photographs and layouts before committing mine geometry.

*Mission seed:* the player must preserve a neutral mine's civilian access, not simply occupy an ore tile. Any introduction of espionage, planted reports or fictional foreign intervention must be flagged as game fiction rather than revisionist history about the real miners.

## 9. RTS readability: history without losing the game

At normal RTS zoom, preserve silhouette logic:
- **Civilian:** public buses, trams, passenger cars, utility vans, rail wagons, construction equipment and mixed pedestrian density—not background war targets.
- **Neutral authority:** credible governmental signs, inspection lights, institutional barriers and patrol routes; neutral palette stays distinguishable from any playable-faction colour.
- **Atlantic Concord / Red Directorate / Iron Ascendancy:** gameplay asymmetry comes from the existing faction designs, not a literal transplant of NATO, Warsaw Pact or historical fascist/communist uniforms. Period reference informs material quality, radios, signage and silhouettes without asserting these fictional factions existed historically.
- **Meridian Pact:** practical light trucks, markets, transportable equipment and modular trade yards; its inexpensive sabotage and fast light scouting are established fiction, not a documented feature of historical merchants.

A practical material study for original assets might contrast worn concrete, painted metal, glazed tile, red tram enamel, corrugated industrial cladding, winter salt, brown newsprint and daylight-glared glass. These are *proposed art assets* requiring individual reference checks, not verified period colour standards.

## 10. Audio and signal design

Reference modes: period radio addresses; urban tram squeal, bell and overhead-wire hum; train brakes; public telephones and switching; mechanical typewriters, document stampers, printing rooms; industrial sirens; conference interpretation; winter air and crowds. Historic recordings should **not** be sampled or shipped absent permission. Commission original diegetic audio, retain local language variety and acoustic provenance, and avoid flattening six republics into one accent or music style. Research specific hardware and interfaces before depicting any named device as a documented period model.

## 12. Next acquisition / review queue

**P0:** authentic 1984–1989 public transport and street-level photos across Belgrade, Zagreb, Ljubljana, Skopje, Titograd and Priština; map-level dated sources for rail/ports; actual issue/page scans and industrial imagery.

**P1:** 1980s factories, vehicles, JNA/civil-defence visual manuals and government forms with item/rights provenance; Sarajevo Olympic press materials and photo records; food packaging, cinema signs, consumer shops, markets and offices.

**P2:** period audio, film, oral testimony, alternative music, regional youth dress, trams/buses, languages and *local* signage. Prefer one strongly verified object per region to a hundred loosely labelled postcards.

## Asset acceptance checklist

To move from this atlas to a production asset: record archive ID(s), image/text date, geography, observed fact, original design interpretation, legal status of *any* pixels/samples copied, and a reviewer comparison. A historian-approved visual shape is not necessarily a redistribution-cleared graphic.
