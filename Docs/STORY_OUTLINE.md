# POLITIKA — Story Outline

**Status:** Core narrative canon; campaign structure, characters, and mission titles are provisional  
**Period:** 1984–1989, in an alternate-history Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia  
**Genre:** Political conspiracy, nuclear brinkmanship, Cold War espionage, military strategy, and national collapse  
**Spoiler warning:** This document discloses the central mystery and the canonical ending.  
**Related material:** [The Unaligned](../Factions/TheUnaligned/description.md) · [Faction descriptions](../Factions) · [UI overview](POLITIKA_UI_OVERVIEW.md)

## 1. Central Premise

POLITIKA tells the story of a state whose greatest symbol of unity has been dead for years—and whose government cannot admit it.

In 1984, Josip Broz Tito is assassinated when agents of **the Iron Ascendancy** arrange for his food to be poisoned. A very small circle at the center of **the Unaligned**, the non-playable faction representing Yugoslavia's federal authorities, discovers the killing. Convinced that confirmation of Tito's death would invite foreign interference, fracture the federation, and provoke domestic conflict, the conspirators conceal his death.

A **body double** impersonates Tito at carefully controlled public appearances. A separate **voice double** delivers recorded proclamations, radio addresses, and propaganda. Official communications present Tito as alive, although increasingly secluded and frail. In reality, federal decisions attributed to him are drafted and authorized by an inner circle acting in his name.

The original assassination is the most closely guarded secret of the Unaligned. Maintaining the deception becomes a matter of state security. Officials, witnesses, journalists, soldiers, and civilians who approach the truth encounter intimidation, surveillance, disinformation, detention, and, in the darkest cases, lethal suppression. Most Yugoslav civil servants, soldiers, and citizens are unaware of the conspiracy; the Unaligned must never be portrayed as a single organization in which every member knows the truth.

Across four playable geopolitical factions, seemingly unrelated military operations, intelligence disputes, infrastructure crises, and regional confrontations reveal fragments of the same underlying mystery. By 1989, the accumulated evidence can no longer be contained. The world learns that Tito was murdered in 1984, that the public Tito was a substitute, and that federal authorities knowingly concealed the truth.

A second, parallel campaign thread concerns an effort to **prevent the Iron Ascendancy from obtaining a particular nuclear weapon**. The Atlantic Concord, Red Directorate, and Iron Ascendancy are the three nuclear-armed playable powers; the Iron Ascendancy seeks to acquire a weapon not presently under its control. The Concord and Directorate have rival reasons to stop the acquisition. The non-nuclear Meridian Pact is deliberately disengaged from this nuclear contest: it receives no dedicated nuclear storyline or acquisition/prevention objectives. The wider Yugoslav authorities can respond to threats within their territory without becoming a playable nuclear power.

The disclosure destroys the credibility of the institutions that claimed to preserve national unity. In combination with long-running economic distress, competing republican interests, nationalist mobilization, foreign intervention, and the militarization of political disputes, this crisis precipitates the disintegration of Yugoslavia into war. **The secret is revealed and the federation descends into war in the canonical ending; players determine how the crisis unfolds locally, who survives, and what their faction gains or loses—not whether the fundamental revelation occurs.**

## 2. Historical Divergence and Rules of the Setting

POLITIKA is explicitly **alternate history**, not a depiction of actual events. In real history, Tito died on **4 May 1980**. In the game's timeline, he survives the illness that killed him in reality and continues, increasingly intermittently, as the public figurehead of the federation until his assassination in 1984. This survival is the foundational historical divergence, not an overlooked inconsistency.

The setting draws on the historical Yugoslav federation, the six socialist republics, the autonomous provinces of Vojvodina and Kosovo, the League of Communists, workers' self-management, the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA), Territorial Defence, and the doctrine of nonalignment. Its four outside powers—the Atlantic Concord, Red Directorate, Iron Ascendancy, and Meridian Pact—are fictionalized geopolitical forces. Their covert activities and the 1984–1989 events described here are invented for the game.

The constitutional and regional complexities of Yugoslavia remain important even while this timeline diverges. Tito's continued public existence has delayed, displaced, or reshaped some political transitions that occurred after 1980 in real history. The narrative must not simply assume that every actual event of the decade happens on its historical date.

The story treats the final war as the result of interacting political and strategic causes, **not** as an automatic reaction by any population to a single revelation. The conspiracy is the decisive catalyst in this fiction, but its consequences depend on the existing weaknesses of the federation and the choices made by powerful actors.

## 3. The Assassination: 1984

### The operation

An inner intelligence cell of the Iron Ascendancy orders Tito's death to weaken the only indigenous authority capable of keeping rival external powers at arm's length. The cell arranges for poison to be introduced into food served at a restricted meal. The specific substance, preparation method, and operational details are not central to the story and should not become an instructional gameplay mechanic.

The conspirators expect the assassination to provoke a leadership crisis. Instead, Yugoslav security officials isolate the incident, seal the medical and catering records, restrict information leaving the residence, and quietly remove surviving personnel from public view. The Iron Ascendancy has killed Tito but does **not** control the response. The cover-up is an independent decision made by a small Yugoslav inner circle.

### The first lie

The security leadership believes that acknowledging the death would invite immediate challenges to federal authority. A provisional emergency measure—working title **Protocol Continuity**—becomes permanent. A physically similar substitute is prepared for distant, brief, strictly scripted appearances; another performer supplies Tito's voice for radio and recorded messages.

The doubles are **two distinct people**, with separate handlers, vulnerabilities, and moral agency. Neither actually governs. Public access is increasingly restricted under the explanation of age, illness, and security. Speeches are recorded, meetings occur without independent observers, images are selectively released, and crucial decisions are issued through officials claiming Tito's authorization. These restrictions generate rumors instead of eliminating them.

### The evidence that survives

The cover-up fails to destroy every trace. Several independent lines of evidence remain:

- a restricted medical record establishing the true date of death;
- discrepancies in kitchen, delivery, and security logs from the final meal;
- orders creating the substitution arrangement and moving the doubles;
- original audio recordings and edited broadcast masters;
- observations from witnesses whose accounts differ from official statements;
- records linking the Iron Ascendancy's assassination cell to the operation.

No single document should be sufficient to settle the mystery. The eventual public revelation succeeds because multiple sources corroborate one another.

## 4. The Unaligned and Its Inner Secret

**The Unaligned remains an exclusively AI/NPC-controlled faction.** It represents the Yugoslav federal administration, armed forces, security institutions, and territorial authorities; it does not become a fifth playable campaign.

Only a small **Continuity Directorate** (provisional name), embedded within the upper federal and security apparatus, knows the complete story. Its members disagree about the morality and duration of the concealment. Some see themselves as preventing civil war; others gradually use the fiction of Tito's continuing authority to preserve their own power.

The body double begins as a guarded asset but becomes an increasingly dangerous witness. The voice double can reproduce the state's preferred narrative but cannot endlessly imitate a living political leader without contradiction. Medical staff, an archivist, a broadcaster, and security personnel form a wider perimeter of partial knowledge. Certain officers suspect wrongdoing without knowing the full account.

The Unaligned's behavior is therefore **layered**:

1. **Ordinary administration:** civilian institutions, infrastructure, policing, and JNA or Territorial Defence formations carry out normal duties and may respond neutrally to players.
2. **Heightened security:** restricted records, suspicious broadcasts, threatened witnesses, or intrusions near sensitive facilities trigger inspections, surveillance, asset seizures, and targeted deployments.
3. **Continuity protection:** the inner circle authorizes covert recovery of evidence, witness suppression, communications blackouts, and exceptional military orders while attempting to disguise these as routine security measures.
4. **Institutional fracture:** as credibility erodes, different federal, republican, provincial, military, and municipal authorities refuse orders, defect, negotiate, or pursue competing priorities.

The concealment should have consequences for innocent people and for institutions that did not originate it. It should also produce dissent within the authorities: not every member of the Unaligned must approve of censorship or violence.

## 5. The Four Playable Factions

The player's chosen faction determines what intelligence is available, which actions are possible, and why the same strategic incident matters. Each campaign explores part of the shared Tito conspiracy, but none rewrites the canonical truth. A **secondary nuclear-weapons storyline** belongs to the three principal military rivals only: the Concord and Directorate pursue prevention, while the Ascendancy pursues acquisition. The Meridian Pact is playable in the main conspiracy and commercial story but has **no stake in, and no dedicated missions for, the nuclear subplot**.

| Faction | Initial position | Relationship to the secret | Principal campaign dilemma |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atlantic Concord** | Preserve regional stability, strategic access, and influence while resisting rivals | Detects anomalies through diplomatic reporting, intercepted signals, and conflicting imagery; does not initially know the truth | Expose a fraudulent government despite the danger of war, or prioritize containment and geopolitical advantage |
| **Red Directorate** | Expand influence through ideological contacts, security cooperation, and infrastructure | Suspects a hidden struggle within Yugoslav leadership; seeks records and insiders to understand its significance | Use the crisis to secure leverage or recognize that coercive intervention can destroy the sovereignty it claims to respect |
| **Iron Ascendancy** | Destabilize Yugoslavia and capitalize on the removal of its unifying leader | Its assassination cell knows Tito was killed, but most military and political ranks lack the full evidence; its leadership discovers the cover-up is a potential means of blackmail | Hide responsibility, exploit the substitution, or accelerate the federation's destruction even at the cost of exposure |
| **Meridian Pact** | Defend strategic autonomy, maintain economic ties, and resist domination by major blocs | Learns through independent diplomatic, commercial, and personal networks; initially treats the reports cautiously | Protect witnesses and self-determination without becoming another outside power deciding Yugoslavia's future |

The nuclear distinction is deliberately specific: all three principal rivals are **nuclear powers** in POLITIKA's fictional setting, but possession of an arsenal does not mean the Iron Ascendancy already controls **the particular additional weapon** targeted in this subplot. The origin, distinguishing strategic value, custodians, and final disposition of that weapon are still to be authored. It must not be assumed to be a normally purchasable Black Market listing.

No faction should receive an omniscient briefing at the beginning. Even the Iron Ascendancy's broader command structure may know only that an important operation succeeded, not who holds surviving proof. A small number of insiders can know the truth while their field commanders operate on incomplete or deliberately distorted instructions.

## 6. Story Structure: 1984–1989

The following five acts establish **the shared timeline**. Individual faction campaigns revisit certain crises from different perspectives. The mission examples are pitches, not a locked level count.

### Prologue — The Empty Chair (1984)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** An early Iron Ascendancy intelligence assessment identifies an opportunity to acquire an additional nuclear weapon through a covert operation. Its provenance and present custodian remain unknown to most field commanders. The Concord and Directorate are not yet aware of the precise threat.

**Public story:** Tito suffers a temporary health setback and cancels engagements. The state emphasizes continuity, vigilance, and unity.

**Actual events:** The assassination succeeds. A security quarantine conceals the death, the first documents disappear, and the doubles are installed. The Iron Ascendancy watches for the expected leadership vacuum and is puzzled when public announcements continue in Tito's name.

**Player-facing experience:** An ordinary-looking security emergency interrupts a border, communications, or convoy operation. Players receive orders whose strategic purpose is unexplained. Contradictory timestamps and an unusually aggressive Yugoslav response provide the first hints, without revealing the central twist.

**Example RTS situation:** Hold or intercept a vital transport corridor as federal units seal access to an unknown consignment; completing the tactical objective reveals only part of its significance.

### Act I — One Voice, One Country (1984–1985)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** Signals and restricted military movements lead the Concord and Directorate to suspect that the Ascendancy is attempting to obtain control of a particular nuclear weapon. Their competing counterintelligence efforts begin separately and need not reveal the Tito conspiracy.

**Public story:** A frail but living Tito continues to endorse national unity through scheduled appearances and increasingly frequent broadcasts.

**Hidden movement:** The Continuity Directorate consolidates control of official communications, eliminates conflicting records, and tests how far it can govern through the absent leader's image. The first frightened witnesses attempt to disappear or contact outsiders.

**Campaign developments:** Rival factions are drawn into disputes over state television, relay stations, ports, border checkpoints, and government records. Each assumes a different conventional explanation for the Unaligned's heightened sensitivity.

**Possible mission themes:** Defend a broadcast relay; escort a diplomatic convoy through an unexplained exclusion zone; recover a missing archive shipment; evacuate a threatened technical specialist.

**End-of-act discovery:** Tito's recent public addresses contain verifiable inconsistencies. The player learns that some appearances or messages have been manufactured, but not necessarily that he is dead.

### Act II — The Man Behind the Curtain (1986)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** The rival nuclear powers discover that the suspected acquisition is more concrete than a rumor. Protective surveillance, misinformation, and competing attempts to secure a witness or transport record create conventional RTS operations around the threat. The Meridian Pact does not participate in this chain of missions.

**Public story:** The presidency remains stable; rumors of illness and impersonation are dismissed as hostile propaganda.

**Hidden movement:** The body double resists tighter confinement, the voice double fears becoming disposable, and officials disagree about whether to end the deception. The Iron Ascendancy identifies opportunities to threaten the conspirators while hiding its own role in the assassination.

**Campaign developments:** Intelligence operations intersect with conventional clashes around railway junctions, industrial zones, airfields, and communications facilities. Partial evidence passes between rivals, sometimes as bait or deliberate misinformation.

**Possible mission themes:** Capture a signal archive without destroying it; prevent a witness extraction; defend a hospital record transfer; recover an intercepted broadcast master.

**End-of-act discovery:** Credible evidence establishes that **two separate substitutes** are involved. Whether Tito is gravely ill, imprisoned, or dead remains unresolved to most protagonists.

### Act III — A Federation of Secrets (1987)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** A planned transfer or change in custody creates the first serious opportunity for interdiction. Concord and Directorate forces may reluctantly deconflict locally while remaining strategic opponents. Ascendancy forces attempt to maintain secrecy and access; conventional military clashes arise over routes and installations rather than nuclear explosions.

**Public story:** Economic problems, disputes over federal power, demonstrations, and rumors are blamed on sabotage, foreign interference, and regional opportunism.

**Hidden movement:** Security operations justified as protecting national unity begin to damage it. Local authorities resent unexplained commands. A dissenting inner-circle official tries to preserve an authentic account of the assassination before it can be destroyed.

**Campaign developments:** Strategic objectives become politically contradictory. A faction may win territory but lose access to cooperative institutions; rescuing a witness may expose civilians to retaliation; striking a federal installation may destroy the very evidence being sought.

**Possible mission themes:** Secure a printing facility; establish a protected route for an archivist; prevent the destruction of a restricted medical repository; navigate a split between federal and local forces.

**End-of-act discovery:** The original Tito died in **1984**, not merely vanished from public life. The surviving files suggest assassination rather than natural causes. Only a few characters yet know the killer's identity.

### Act IV — The Republics Begin to Break (1988)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** Escalating instability makes security guarantees less reliable, raising the risk that the Ascendancy could obtain the sought weapon. The two countervailing powers intensify efforts to deny access while disagreeing over who should ultimately hold custody. The Unaligned reacts to threats against Yugoslav sovereignty through its ordinary and emergency AI systems, not as a nuclear-armed player faction.

**Public story:** Confidence in federal pronouncements deteriorates; rival political leaders dispute emergency measures; the Unaligned publicly calls for order and unity.

**Hidden movement:** The Directorate is divided between ending the lie and intensifying suppression. The Iron Ascendancy uses both disinformation and genuine evidence to discredit the federation while concealing its own responsibility. Other factions consider whether disclosure would restore legitimacy or produce a strategic catastrophe.

**Campaign developments:** Emergency deployments, strikes, transport disruption, regional political crises, and escalating armed standoffs transform previously neutral territory into contested space. Some JNA and Territorial Defence units still act for federal stability; others receive conflicting orders or refuse them.

**Possible mission themes:** Protect civilian evacuation routes during a security crackdown; seize a transmitter without damaging its evidence; keep a contested transport corridor open; extract a double or former handler who can testify.

**End-of-act discovery:** Documents connect the poisoning to an Iron Ascendancy operation, and signed federal orders establish the scale of the subsequent cover-up. The truth is now demonstrable—but can still be blocked from reaching the public.

### Act V — The Last Broadcast (1989)

**Parallel nuclear thread:** The acquisition-versus-interdiction contest reaches its operational climax as military authority fractures. Dedicated Concord and Directorate missions focus on preventing transfer to Ascendancy control; the Ascendancy campaign attempts to complete it. The precise outcome and the disposition of the weapon are **not yet locked**. This subplot must not displace the fixed revelation of Tito's murder or the canonical Yugoslav collapse.

**Public story:** Federal broadcasts insist that the head of state remains alive and that competing allegations are coordinated foreign fabrications.

**Hidden movement:** Survivors from the assassination, the doubles program, and the archive converge. The inner circle attempts one final communications lockdown. The Iron Ascendancy's surviving conspirators try to redirect blame. Multiple factions race to control the timing, framing, and distribution of the evidence.

**Campaign developments:** The culminating operations concern simultaneous access to communications networks, protection or capture of crucial witnesses, and control of strategic institutions amid growing military defections and local mobilizations.

**Possible final missions:**
- **The Archive:** Recover an intact chain of records rather than simply destroying a target facility.
- **Two Voices:** Secure or confront the separate body and voice doubles as the official narrative begins to fail.
- **The Last Frequency:** Control the principal transmitter and regional relay network while rival powers attempt suppression, forgery, or seizure.
- **The Day After:** Respond to the collapse of federal authority through evacuation, protection of infrastructure, defensive battles, or faction-specific strategic gains.

**Canonical revelation:** In 1989, corroborated records, testimony, and recordings reach the public through multiple channels. The broadcasts establish that Tito was killed in 1984, that the government substituted a body and voice double, and that the Unaligned's inner circle concealed the murder. The Iron Ascendancy's role in the assassination is also exposed, though competing actors dispute responsibility for subsequent crimes.

**Canonical ending:** Emergency claims of authority lose legitimacy; republican and regional institutions challenge federal orders; segments of the security apparatus fracture; foreign factions exploit the crisis; armed clashes spread and Yugoslavia disintegrates into war. The last campaign operations make clear that the revelation has not liberated the country from violence. It has removed the political fiction that concealed deep conflicts without resolving them.

## 7. Secondary Storyline — The Nuclear Weapon

The primary story concerns the concealed assassination of Tito; the **secondary story concerns prevention of a specific nuclear weapon from passing into Iron Ascendancy hands**. Both unfold during 1984–1989 in the same geopolitical theater. They may share transport routes, facilities, intelligence contacts, and tactical engagements, but one is not secretly a substitute for the other. The player's pursuit of the nuclear objective must not erase the central conspiracy.

### Nuclear alignment and motivations

| Faction | Nuclear status in this fictional setting | Participation in the secondary story |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlantic Concord** | Nuclear-armed power | Tries to prevent Ascendancy acquisition to preserve deterrence and prevent escalation; may disagree with Directorate proposals for custody and verification |
| **Red Directorate** | Nuclear-armed power | Pursues its own interdiction, safeguards, and strategic leverage, independently of—or temporarily alongside—the Concord |
| **Iron Ascendancy** | Nuclear-armed power | The prospective acquirer of a **particular additional nuclear weapon**, seeking new coercive or operational leverage that its existing arsenal does not provide |
| **Meridian Pact** | **Non-nuclear power** | **Does not care about or engage in this subplot as a strategic objective**; no dedicated nuclear missions, nuclear research tree, or Black Market nuclear-weapons trade |
| **The Unaligned** | Non-nuclear, NPC-only Yugoslav authority | May enforce security, defend its territory, investigate incursions, or respond to an emergency; it neither pursues the player nuclear plot nor becomes a nuclear faction |

### Narrative and design boundaries

- **Acquisition versus prevention:** the Iron Ascendancy is seeking to obtain an identifiable weapon not already in its custody; the Atlantic Concord and Red Directorate want to stop it, for different political reasons.
- **The weapon's particulars remain open:** its origin, physical location, custody chain, and strategic distinction from existing arsenals will be settled in later narrative design. Avoid claiming its acquisition would mark the Ascendancy's first nuclear capability.
- **Two plots, one campaign world:** shared intelligence operations, border corridors, and crises may connect them, but the assassination secret remains the primary plot. Nuclear intelligence need not reveal Tito's death, and Tito evidence need not solve the nuclear plot.
- **No Meridian participation:** the Pact's trading, mercenary, and Black Market mechanics remain focused on conventional equipment, intelligence, logistics, and contracts. Nuclear weapons are never ordinary purchasable or auctionable inventory, and Meridian receives no nuclear side missions.
- **RTS expression:** reconnaissance, protection of sensitive locations, securing or disrupting a guarded transfer, preventing access, escort or evacuation, and neutral-force escalation. Use conventional tactical objectives rather than instructions for building, arming, handling, or detonating a nuclear device.
- **Deterrence without a mandatory detonation:** the subplot generates urgency through potential strategic escalation. It does not require a nuclear strike, tactical nuclear combat unit, or mass-casualty spectacle.
- **Open resolution:** success/failure of individual missions and the ultimate status of this particular weapon remain design decisions until authored. Neither outcome can avert the canonical 1989 exposure of the assassination and Yugoslavia's descent into war.

### Character and campaign opportunities

Provisional roles include a Concord nonproliferation coordinator, a Directorate security officer, an Ascendancy acquisition commander, and a Yugoslav officer attempting to protect a restricted facility without possessing the full geopolitical context. No Meridian nuclear intermediary should be added simply to reuse the Black Market mechanic.

The subplot supports a second source of pressure even when the player has already inferred Tito's fate: some battles concern whether a weapon changes hands, while others concern whether a historical crime becomes public. Keeping these stakes distinct is essential to pacing.

## 8. Principal Character Functions

Names, biographies, and personal identities remain open for subsequent writing. These roles support a character-driven story without replacing the RTS perspective:

- **The Continuity Director:** Senior federal official who authorizes the first deception as an emergency safeguard and becomes increasingly implicated in coercion.
- **The Body Double:** An ordinary person trapped inside a celebrated public identity; his physical resemblance makes him valuable, but his actual voice and choices remain his own.
- **The Voice Double:** A performer or announcer whose recorded statements are treated as sovereign authority; possesses uniquely revealing original tapes.
- **The Security Commander:** Implements directives across the federation, initially believing the measures to be necessary for stability; may question the orders as civilian harm rises.
- **The Archivist:** Protects the original chronology and chain of custody, making corroborated evidence possible.
- **The Witness:** A surviving medical or service employee who knows what happened during the final meal but lacks the documents proving the conspiracy.
- **The Ascendancy Handler:** Participated in the 1984 operation; tries to turn an assassination into long-term geopolitical leverage while avoiding attribution.
- **The Broadcaster:** Faces the difference between transmitting official orders and knowingly deceiving the public.
- **The Regional Officer:** Embodies the growing conflict between federal chain of command and local political accountability.

These characters should disagree for intelligible reasons. The doubles are not merely props, and the journalist, witness, or dissenter should not exist only to be rescued or killed.

## 9. Narrative Delivery Through RTS Gameplay

POLITIKA's established UI direction rules out mandatory cinematics. The story should therefore advance through **mission briefings, field reports, radio exchanges, objective changes, recovered documents, period-style news bulletins, and debriefings**. Tactical play remains continuous and central.

### Information is a strategic resource

Campaign progression can track three distinct narrative states without requiring a complex branching story engine:

- **Evidence:** What documents, recordings, and testimony the faction possesses and whether they have been corroborated.
- **Nuclear threat status (three principal rivals only):** What is known about the additional sought weapon, whether access or transfer is imminent, and whether an interdiction objective has succeeded. This is distinct from assassination evidence and not a Meridian-specific progression track.
- **Exposure:** Which authorities, rival factions, or civilian audiences have received credible information.
- **Stability:** The degree of institutional cooperation, unrest, security escalation, and command cohesion in a region.

These are narrative design concepts rather than confirmed economic resources or final UI statistics. Players should never see omniscient information that their faction has not acquired.

### Missions express political choices through tactical objectives

A mission should have an understandable military objective and a story consequence. The player may capture an archive intact or destroy it; protect a transmitter or silence it; extract a witness or abandon the route; cooperate with federal forces or violate territorial sovereignty. Optional objectives can preserve records, minimize civilian losses, maintain diplomatic access, or prevent an escalation. Winning a battle does not always mean solving the political problem.

The Unaligned's AI response can intensify when players investigate forbidden areas or transport sensitive evidence. At the same time, ordinary federal troops may oppose the Iron Ascendancy or cooperate with a foreign faction for legitimate defensive purposes. Not every encounter with Unaligned units should be hostile.

### Campaign convergence

All four campaigns share the **same fixed core facts and 1989 revelation**. Only three of those campaigns—the Concord, Directorate, and Ascendancy—have dedicated nuclear-subplot operations. They differ in perspective, access, mission order, relationships, and the local consequences of player performance. Important events occurring offscreen in one faction's campaign can become playable missions in another. This preserves a coherent setting without requiring four contradictory histories.

Defeats or optional outcomes may alter the condition of a town, the survival of supporting characters, the integrity of an archive, the reputation of a faction, or the intensity of the final battles. The world-changing disclosure and outbreak of war remain canonical.

## 10. Reveal Pacing and Dramatic Principles

1. **1984–1985: Anomaly.** The audience sees an unexplained security response and unnatural control of public appearances.
2. **1986: Substitution.** Evidence suggests that Tito's image and voice are fabricated; his actual condition is unclear.
3. **1987: Death.** Independent testimony and medical records establish the 1984 death.
4. **1988: Responsibility.** The player's faction can uncover the foreign assassination plot and federal complicity in the cover-up.
5. **1989: Public proof.** Corroborated evidence becomes impossible to suppress; the state's legitimacy collapses.

Some campaigns may learn individual facts earlier than others, especially the Iron Ascendancy's limited insider view. The dramatic arc must still preserve the distinction between **suspicion, private knowledge, credible proof, and public acceptance**.

Recurring motifs include the empty chair at meetings, a television appearance without unscripted questions, a radio voice heard after the speaker's death, duplicate signatures, sealed corridors, missing archival reels, and official demands for unity that generate further distrust.

## 11. Ending, Tone, and Themes

POLITIKA is a tragic political thriller with no uncomplicated savior faction. The Iron Ascendancy's assassination creates the central wound, but the Unaligned's concealment turns it into a constitutional and moral disaster. Other powers compound the crisis when they treat Yugoslavia as an arena for strategic competition rather than a sovereign society.

The principal themes are **truth versus stability**, **legitimacy versus coercion**, **state continuity versus institutional honesty**, **the difference between a leader and the image of a leader**, **foreign manipulation**, and **the human cost of geopolitical strategy**.

The ending should not imply that Yugoslavia's peoples were destined for war or that a single secret mechanically explains social conflict. In this invented timeline, exposing the lie removes the last widely accepted symbol of unity while multiple factions and domestic power centers choose escalation over compromise. Military victory may give a faction leverage, but no campaign can undo the national tragedy.

The last narrative image should be achievable without a cinematic: a command map fills with conflicting orders and expanding conflict markers while the final official broadcast is replaced by an independently verified announcement. The player still commands units, but the voice that once claimed to speak for the entire federation is gone.

## 12. Canon Locked / Details Open

### Locked foundations

- Tito survives past his real-world 1980 death in the alternate timeline.
- The Iron Ascendancy assassinates him through poisoned food in **1984**.
- A small inner circle of the Unaligned conceals the death to maintain public stability and federal authority.
- **Separate body and voice doubles** sustain the illusion of a living Tito.
- The Unaligned is **NPC-only** and suppresses knowledge of the conspiracy, though most of its institutions and personnel do not know the full truth.
- The Atlantic Concord, Red Directorate, Iron Ascendancy, and Meridian Pact are the four playable factions.
- The **primary storyline** revolves around the secret from 1984 to 1989.
- The **secondary storyline** centers on preventing the Iron Ascendancy from obtaining a particular additional nuclear weapon. The Concord and Directorate try to prevent it; the Ascendancy attempts acquisition.
- The Atlantic Concord, Red Directorate, and Iron Ascendancy are the three **nuclear-armed playable powers**. The Meridian Pact is **non-nuclear** and uninterested in this subplot.
- The Unaligned remains NPC-only and is not a nuclear-armed playable power. Nuclear weapons are not ordinary Black Market items.
- The cover-up becomes public in **1989**; Yugoslavia disintegrates into war.

### To settle during campaign writing

- The exact date, venue, and immediate witnesses of the 1984 assassination.
- Official titles and identities of the Continuity Directorate and principal characters.
- How often the doubles appear, their motivations, and whether either survives 1989.
- The precise sequence by which the original medical records and assassination evidence survive.
- Campaign mission count, region order, and available tactical resources.
- The sought nuclear weapon's provenance, custody, strategic significance, final disposition, and the detailed interdiction mission outcomes.
- Which optional outcomes change character fates and regional conditions.
- The specific chain of institutional failures and military confrontations that turns disclosure into open war.

**One-sentence story promise:** *In a divided Yugoslavia, rival nuclear powers race to deny the Iron Ascendancy a coveted weapon while four playable factions uncover the truth about a leader who has been dead since 1984—and in 1989, that truth tears the country apart.*
